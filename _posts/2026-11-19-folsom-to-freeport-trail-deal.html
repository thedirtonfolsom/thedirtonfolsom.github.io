A deal to connect a 48-mile trail from Folsom to Freeport

The state, Sacramento County and the City of Sacramento signed an agreement on Friday, Nov. 20, to join two big trails into one continuous route. The 32-mile American River Bike Trail, which starts in Folsom, would link up with the 16-mile Sacramento River Parkway, which runs south to Freeport. The announcement was made at Sutter's Landing Regional Park in Sacramento.

What's missing: About 7.4 miles of trail still need to be built, all in Sacramento:

  • Four gaps totaling about 2.4 miles, including a half-mile stretch west of Sutter's Landing, a short link near Old Sacramento, a piece through Miller Park and a 1.5-mile gap in the Little Pocket neighborhood.
  • About one more mile of the Two Rivers Trail and about 4 miles of new Sacramento River Parkway trail.

The money: The state is putting in $2 million from Proposition 4, the climate bond voters passed in 2024. That is not enough to build everything. It goes first to planning and to buying land, because some of the route crosses private property. No completion date has been set.

Why it matters for Folsom: Folsom sits at the eastern end of the route, so once the gaps are filled, local riders could bike from Folsom to Freeport, south of downtown Sacramento, on trail the whole way. Folsom is also closing its own big gap: the city is designing a bike and walking path under Highway 50 near Placerville Road, which would connect the trails north of the freeway with the ones to the south. When the council approved that project in December 2025, construction was expected to start in summer 2027.

Sources: Folsom Times · CBS13 on the Highway 50 undercrossing

Explainer: What is the American River Bike Trail?

Its official name is the Jedediah Smith Memorial Trail. It runs 32 miles along the American River, from Folsom to where the American meets the Sacramento River in downtown Sacramento. It goes through the American River Parkway, about 7,000 acres of protected land along the river. Sacramento County runs the lower 23 miles of the parkway, from the Hazel Avenue bridge to the rivers' meeting point.

Source: Folsom Times

2

Future Folsom forum: a strong economy, but stuck housing

Business and government leaders met Nov. 18 at Lakeside Church in Folsom for the annual Future Folsom Economic Forum, hosted by the business group Choose Folsom. The main speaker was economist Chris Thornberg of Beacon Economics.

On housing: Thornberg said California's core problem is that it doesn't build enough homes. He also pointed to "golden handcuffs" (see the explainer below), and said building an apartment in California costs about twice as much as a similar one in Texas.

On Folsom: He put Folsom's typical home price just under $800,000 and its median household income around $120,000. The city has more than 41,000 jobs. He flagged falling residential building permits in Folsom as a concern.

Next door: Sacramento County's economic development director, Crystal Bethke, said Hazel Avenue near Highway 50 is set to be widened in 2027–2028, and that more than 16,000 homes are planned in master-planned communities along the corridor. Rancho Cordova City Manager Micah Runner said his city added 734 new homes in the past year.

Why it matters: Folsom Ranch, south of Highway 50, is where the city plans about 11,000 new homes. Fewer permits means fewer new homes coming to market, and with existing owners staying put, the supply of homes for sale stays tight.

Sources: Folsom Times · City of Folsom on Folsom Ranch

Explainer: What are "golden handcuffs"?

It's the term for homeowners who feel locked into their homes by a low mortgage rate. Thornberg said about 70% of people with a mortgage have a rate of 5% or less. Selling would mean taking out a new loan at today's higher rates, so many owners don't move, and fewer homes come up for sale.

Source: Folsom Times

$1 million from two health companies to fix up three Folsom sports sites

The Folsom City Council approved sponsorship deals on Nov. 10 with Dignity Health and Western Health Advantage. Each will pay the city $100,000 a year for five years, $1 million in all, as part of the city's Healthy Families, Healthy Folsom program.

  • Andy Morin Sports Complex (first priority): new indoor soccer turf in spring 2027, batting cages in winter 2027 and scoreboards in spring 2028. Sports courts are also on the list.
  • Econome Family Park: upkeep of the synthetic soccer fields and new fencing around the edge, in spring 2027.
  • Steve Miklos Aquatic Center: upgrades to the water slide tower and equipment, planned for fall 2027.

What the sponsors get: Signs with their names near the upgraded areas, mentions in city publications and social media, and a presence at community events. According to the city staff report, neither company gets naming rights or exclusive use of the facilities.

Why it matters: As Folsom grows, it has more parks and fields to maintain. Deals like this cover repairs that would otherwise come out of the city's own budget.

Source: Sacramento Bee via Yahoo
